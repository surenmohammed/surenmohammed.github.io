Research

My research examines the spatial and temporal dynamics of political violence, with a focus on ethnic and communal conflict. I combine quantitative methods, machine learning, and qualitative case studies to understand why violence emerges in some places but not others.

The Spatial Logic of Violence in Communal War: Evidence from Iraq (2003-2009)

Mohammed, Suren J.

Job Market Paper | Expected Submission: Spring 2026

Why does communal violence emerge and peak in some localities but not others? This paper argues that during moments of political upheaval, localities with near demographic parity between rival communal groups experience earlier and more intense communal violence. Using mixed methods combining negative binomial regression, survival analysis, and case studies from Iraq, I show that high-parity districts experienced significantly more violence and earlier onset than low-parity districts. The mechanism works through local security dilemmas: spatial proximity to a sizable rival group amplifies fear and uncertainty, hardens communal identities, and facilitates mobilization for violence.

Dissertation

Explaining the Spatial Logic of Ethnic Violence

Committee: TBD

My dissertation investigates why ethnic and communal violence concentrates in some localities while leaving others relatively peaceful. Drawing on theories of security dilemmas, ethnic geography, and collective action, I develop and test a theory of communal parity that explains both the intensity and timing of violence. The project combines quantitative analysis of conflict data with qualitative case studies to trace the mechanisms linking demographic composition to violent outcomes.
